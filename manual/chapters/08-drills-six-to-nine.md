# The Drills: Six to Nine, the Figures

Four drills share one cast of fifteen hand-drawn figures. They differ in what the
figure does, not in how it is scored.

![One of the fifteen. The scoring rings are printed in the drawing itself.](media/thug04.jpg){width=2.7in}

## What they all have in common

**The rings that score are the rings you can see.** They are printed in the
artwork, and the app reads them out of the ink as polygons rather than guessing
a shape over the top. An early attempt fitted ellipses and rounded rectangles to
them; the printed rings are bottle-shaped and no analytic shape followed them, so
the drawing was taken as the authority instead. What you are scored against and
what you are aiming at are the same line.

**A figure whose rings could not be verified is never drawn at all.** Better a
smaller cast than a figure scored wrongly.

**The cast is variety, not information.** Nothing is scored on which figure
appears. The next one is drawn at random, never the one just shown, and placed
well away from where the last one stood.

**A hit on the figure but outside every ring still shows.** It leaves a dim grey
mark and scores nothing. For a long time such a shot drew nothing at all, which
from the firing line reads as "shots land inside the target and nothing happens".
Note that the head carries a printed 9 and a 7 with no ring behind them: a hit
there is on the figure, not in a scoring zone.

**Holes ride the figure.** A shot that lands on a figure becomes part of it,
stored in the figure's own coordinates and redrawn as it moves and scales, so a
walking target carries its hits with it and takes them away when it leaves.

**Thirty points drops it.** Each scoring hit adds its ring value to a running
total shown beside the figure. At thirty the figure goes down into its own fall
pose for a second, and the next one comes. The total belongs to that figure; the
next one starts from zero.

## 6 - Pop-Up Target

![Two different figures, revealed at the same instant, one per side.](media/mode6.jpg){width=6.9in}

The reaction drill with a figure as the target: a blank screen, an unpredictable
wait, then a figure at an unpredictable place and an unpredictable size, which
stays for a few seconds and leaves whether or not you hit it. Firing before it
appears is a false start and is never scored. It measures the time from the
figure appearing to your first hit, the rings those first hits found, and the
figures that left unengaged.

## 7 - Static Target

![One figure, held, with its running total beside it.](media/mode7.jpg){width=6.9in}

One figure, standing, until you drop it. The counter beside it shows the running
total rather than the value of the last hit -- *"I want you to flash the total
number, not just the number I hit"* -- and the holes stay on the figure while it
stands. A new round is a new figure, and it takes its holes with it.

This is the paper-target readout: where the shots went, what they scored, and how
long the string took.

## 8 - Moving Target

![On the right, a figure holding at a distance. On the left, one that has just been dropped.](media/mode8.jpg){width=6.9in}

A figure walks toward you, holds, and retreats, drifting sideways as it goes.
**The size is the distance**: it grows as it approaches and shrinks as it goes
away. Everything about a cycle is drawn at random -- which figure, how long the
wait, which side it comes from, how far away it starts, how long it approaches,
holds and retreats.

It reports hit rate and score in three distance bands, and the time from each
hold beginning to the first hit.

One detail that matters more than it sounds: a shot is confirmed two camera
frames after the trigger breaks, by which time a walking figure has moved and
grown. The hit test therefore rewinds to where the figure was *and how big it
was* at the moment of the shot. Without that rewind you would have to shoot in
front of the target, which is exactly what the ball drills used to require before
the same fix was applied to them.

## 9 - Animated Target

![The filmed soldier, and the hit counter above him.](media/mode9.jpg){width=6.9in}

A filmed figure, ten seconds of it, moving continuously -- aiming, firing,
crouching and rising. There are no rings printed on him, so he is scored the only
honest way left: a shot inside his silhouette scores, a shot outside it does not,
and the silhouette is the shape you can see rather than a box around it.

**Four hits in the head or centre mass drop him.** A hit anywhere else on him
scores ten points but does not advance the counter, and the counter above him
says `HIT - NO COUNT` for a moment so you know why. He is never marked with
holes: he moves every frame, and holes on him would be new lit area on every one
of them. The counter carries the feedback instead.

When he goes down he plays his own fall, rests for a second, and comes back.
